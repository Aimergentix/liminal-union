## Early ages

In deep prehistory, certain Halfelven kindreds on the southern marches of the great forests turned away from the elder Elvish order. They rejected bloodline hierarchy, hereditary arcane privilege, and the growing authority of gifted persons. These kindreds moved south into the borderlands and began to form a different civic order.

From this break came the first Liminal communities: settlements bound by oath, witness, and compact rather than by lordship or sacred descent.

## The Sundering, the Eighth-Day Severance, and the Bound of the Dark Forest

The departure from the elder Elvish courts was not merely political; it struck at the root of Elvish life. In the old order, Elves drew longevity and arcane grace from their bond to the Faerie plane. When that tether was broken or corrupted, the body began to wither and consume itself unless anchored anew or sustained from without.

Those who fled the Elvish courts divided along two irreconcilable paths:

The **Halfelven founders** chose mortal finitude, civic law, and disciplined craft. Through early alchemical and metallurgical inquiry, their physicians and artificers instituted the **Eighth-Day Severance** — a solemn threshold rite by which every newborn child is sheltered for seven days from arcane influence while receiving a dampening trace of **Zoelite** through the mother's milk (*smelting-milk*), and on the eighth day is bathed in warm liquefied **Akosite** (*the Catharite bath*) and sealed at the unclosed crown of the skull with an Akosite poultice. Though the shock of severance proved fatal to a small fraction of infants whose hearts remained too deeply woven with residual faerie essence, those who endured emerged permanently untethered from the Faerie plane, anchored to mortal life, and entered into the civic rolls as free citizens.

A second faction of exiles — occultist elves who had fled the courts alongside the Halfelves as allies of convenience — refused both the Elven crown and mortal finitude. Rather than accept the Severance, they turned to forbidden blood-ritual to sustain their faerie-severed bodies by outside life-force, becoming the vampiric houses of the Dark Forest. Though the Union abhorred their rites, the two groups faced a common threat from the monarchist task-forces of Evercrown. Out of grim necessity arose **the Treaty of the Living and the Bound** (preserved in Sayyon's foundational charters as the *Dark Forest Charter*): the vampiric houses were recognized as a separate, strictly bounded border state holding the Dark Forest marches against Evercrown. Forbidden to prey upon Union citizens, they contracted with Sayyon for sealed steel containment vessels, filtration tubes, and hunting gear to harvest the blood of borderland megafauna, yielding in exchange condensed arcane-charged mineral residues and stable bio-resonant cells from their ritual reservoirs.

## The founding flight and the law of the open threshold

Sayyon was not built by conquerors or settled nobility. It was raised by those who had come from elsewhere — peoples driven from older lands by hereditary tyranny, resentment-rule, the closed orders of bloodline lords, and the suppression of the lowborn. They arrived across the Black River carrying memory of exclusion and carrying a binding principle: that what they built would treat strangers as they themselves wished to have been treated when they fled.

From this origin the Union drew its first governing principle before any formal ban was written: the threshold must remain open, and must remain fair. The stranger who arrives in need stands under the same law and compact as the settled — with equal claim and equal obligation. The former enemy who surrenders arms and accepts the threshold stands under equal civic protection, not mercy, but full standing. The long annals of Sayyon call this **the law of the open threshold**, and it is treated as foundational, not aspirational. It binds all who dwell under it to the same dignity.

Mutual obligation — to neighbour, to stranger, to the defeated enemy who lays down arms — is written into the civic compact. A **public fund of common provision** was among the earliest Liminal institutions: relief for the destitute, shelter for the displaced, support for those injured in public service. It is funded by collective levy and inspected by public law. The Union does not call this generosity. It calls it the minimum condition of a commonwealth that does not wish to repeat the history of the places its founders fled.

## Ban on Living Spellcraft

As these communities faced war and pressure from neighboring powers, they confronted the problem of sorcery in public life. Their answer was the **Ban on Living Spellcraft**. The Union forbade direct spell-use by mortal persons, mage-castes, grimoires, occult schools, and necromantic practice, while permitting arcane matter governed by craft, inspection, and law.

This distinction became the foundation of Liminal doctrine.

The later annals preserve the **Judgment of the Unsealed Hand** as the crisis that made the distinction unavoidable. In a frontier settlement, a gifted defender set aside witness and council in the name of immediate necessity. The danger was real, and the defender's acts first appeared to save the settlement; yet each remedy demanded a further private command, until no neighbour could question the hand that claimed to protect them. When the settlement endured, its people judged not only the harm that had been done, but the authority that had been claimed. They resolved that no mortal person should wield arcane force beyond public law, however useful the first promise might appear.

## Sayyon and the Union

In time the early settlements raised **Sayyon**, later called **Salem**, at the meeting of river, sea, and caravan-route. Around it a broader union of towns, councils, and peoples took shape. The polity became known as the **Liminal Union**: a commonwealth defined not by blood, but by shared law and public obligation.

## The Halls of Inquiry and public learning

As the early Union communities chose craft over sorcery, they faced a second imperative: knowledge could not be hoarded in grimoires, inherited in bloodlines, or locked behind occult initiation. It had to be made common. The earliest communities established schools open to all children, and in time these grew into the **Halls of Inquiry** in Sayyon — the oldest institutions of higher learning recorded on the Great Continent.

The Halls taught alchemy, metallurgy, law, navigation, civic arts, and the inspection of arcane materials. Over generations, this policy of universal public education produced a people of unusual general literacy, craft competence, and civic judgment. The Union regards this not as a gift of blood or gift of magic, but as the fruit of open institutions sustained across centuries of public obligation.

The doctrine is explicit: knowledge is civic property. An uninstructed citizen is a vulnerability in the commonwealth's fabric equal to an unpatrolled border.

## Arcanite and lawful artifice

Denied living spellcraft, Liminal artificers turned to alchemy, metallurgy, and disciplined craft — a path parallel in spirit to the rune-forges of the Dwarvish holds or the naphtha-compounding of the eastern sands, yet governed throughout by civic charter. Where Dwarvish runesmiths lock elemental force into carved structural marks, Sayyon's foundries learned to smelt reactive minerals and anti-sorcerous crystals directly into the body of the metal itself.

From these furnace-traditions came the Union's canonical alloys:
- **Pyrite**, refined from deep, volatile mineral seams to release a self-sustaining thermal reaction upon impact without a mage's incantation;
- **Kryonite**, an ultra-dense, heat-drinking deep-earth alloy that drains ambient warmth on contact and locks defensive shield-walls;
- **Arcanite**, smelted by binding anti-magical crystals into the metal matrix so that the blade or plate itself disrupts the dark magic sustaining the undead and the primal arcane vitality of draken;
- **Uranite**, housed in sealed officer rods and conductance vessels to steady liminal soldiers under the harsh glare of full Day and the deep shadow of Night;
- **Zoelite** and **Akosite**, refined from deep forest-mountain iron veins where centuries of faerie-touched woodland waters had steeped the ore with anti-venom and purifying properties, serving both in the Eighth-Day Severance and as passive metallic conduits that amplify the detoxifying warmth of **Phoinix Messengers**.

Alongside these alloys arose arcane wargear, bound cores, and the old golem-craft from which the later **G9 Warforms** descend. The Union’s military order matured long before the rise of Wesnoth.

## Annals of trial

The annals of Sayyon do not claim that its first laws were obeyed without failure. Three remembered trials are recited when magistrates, officers, and artificers take public oath.

**The First Threshold Oath** was sworn by the early settlements after a band of defeated enemies laid down arms at their gates. The oath held that protection and obligation must be offered together: the surrendered would not be enslaved or driven into the wild, but neither would they stand outside the common law.

**The Ash-Foundry Judgment** followed the failure of an early bound core in a crowded craft ward. The court did not forbid the craft, nor excuse those who had maintained it poorly. It required inspection, named responsibility, and a public record for every such work. From this judgment came the principle that lawful artifice is not made safe by secrecy, but by answerability.

**The Closing of the River Gate** came in a season of hunger, when frightened officials barred arrivals despite the old compact. The gate was reopened only after bitter dispute and costly provision. Later courts remembered the shame as a warning: an open threshold is most truly tested when grain, shelter, and patience are scarce.

## The Green Isle arrivals and the Scarlet Faerie Stone

When the peoples of the Green Isle came east, they brought with them Orcish pursuit and the designs of the Lich-Lords. In Sayyon’s annals, the Ruby of Fire was remembered as the **Scarlet Faerie Stone**. The Union’s merchants, couriers, and scholars had already marked it as a necromantic danger.

The current docs claim covert Union intervention in keeping the stone out of necromantic hands. The scale and exact mechanism of that intervention are unknown if not in repo/docs.

## After the rise of Wesnoth

With the founding of Wesnoth, the Union encountered a northern state that shared some respect for law yet remained entangled with crown, hierarchy, and tolerated magi. Trade and contact remained possible, but deep trust did not.

## The first compact of the passes

In the years when northern roads were broken by war and toll-lord, a Sayyoni convoy came to a Knalgan mountain gate. It carried neither sermon nor demand for submission, but a sealed charter concerning weights, repairs, passage, and mutual defence. The masters of the forge examined the Union's bound cores; the Union's artificers examined the hold's rune-work. Neither called the other's craft holy. Both required it to answer for its making.

From that exchange came a modest traffic of metals, charcoal, timber, tools, grain, and guarded carriage through the passes. The compact bound no people to another's service, and it did not quiet every quarrel of road, mine, or border. It established only this: where a charter held, a caravan kept faith, and the gatewardens of both sides kept watch, trade could outlast suspicion.

## The Second Sun

The raising of the second sun was taken in Sayyon as proof that mortal spellcraft, even when wielded for high purposes, could reshape the world without lawful restraint. The Union did not answer by rejecting arcane matter. It answered by redoubling its older commitment to lawful containment: Arcanite, bound cores, wargear, warforms, and disciplined mortal craft.
