## The Liminal Union

South of the lands later called Wesnoth, beyond the Black River, stands **Sayyon / Salem**, civic heart of the **Liminal Union**. The Union is an ancient commonwealth of law, thresholds, and disciplined craft: stern in doctrine, open in custom, and shaped by the meeting of many peoples.

Its ruling ideal is simple and severe. No bloodline, shrine, grimoire, or gifted person may stand above civic law.

## Sayyon, city of the threshold

Sayyon, which many northern tongues call Salem, stands where river-road, sea-road, and desert road dispute the same stones. Its gates bear no king's device, but the seals of the courts that keep them. Within its walls lie quays and storeyards, the Halls of Inquiry, foundries, counting-houses, and the old wards where those newly come are first received under the law.

The city is neither a refuge without rule nor a fortress closed against need. A ship may enter by the river-gate only after its cargo is witnessed; a caravan may pass the outer court only after its people and beasts are accounted for. The same record that establishes a merchant's burden may establish a stranger's claim to shelter. Sayyon holds that a threshold is kept not by turning every traveller away, but by knowing who has crossed it, what protection is owed, and what obligation has been accepted.

## The liminal principle

The Union takes its name from the threshold. It values crossings: dawn and dusk, river and shore, memory and reform, old peoples learning to live under one law. It distrusts every order that claims perfection in fixed blood, fixed doctrine, or fixed magical privilege.

To be liminal is not to drift. It is to endure change without surrendering judgment.

## The open threshold

Sayyon was built by those who fled.

Its founders came from older orders where birth determined fate: where the lowborn had no standing before law, where the stranger was turned away by blood-right, where exclusion was doctrine and resentment was policy. They crossed rivers, deserts, and contested marches to reach the threshold of something different. And they vowed that when they had built their commonwealth, it would treat the stranger — and even the former enemy — as they themselves wished to have been treated when they were homeless and fleeing.

The Union remembered this. It made the memory a law.

The **law of the open threshold** holds that those who bind themselves to the commonwealth — whether long-settled or newly arrived, whether once enemy or longtime kin — owe one another equal standing before law, equal claim on the civic compact, and equal obligation to uphold it. The stranger who arrives in need is not given charity; the stranger is received as a potential member of the commonwealth and held to the same law as any settled citizen. The former enemy who surrenders and accepts the threshold stands under full civic protection, not as a subject, but as one bound by the same oath all citizens bear.

A **fund of common provision** sustains the destitute, shelters the displaced, and supports those broken in public service — not from sentiment, but because the commonwealth that abandons its own is not a commonwealth; it is a gang of gatekeepers. This fund is no different in the Union's eyes from the maintenance of roads, walls, or warforms: a public work, inspected by law, belonging to all equally.

The lowborn are not a subject order. They are citizens — with the same standing as any. The compact makes no distinction of birth before the law, and the Halls of Inquiry admit by merit, not by purse or bloodline.

This severity — the insistence that the Union's civic bonds hold equal and firm, without sentiment but also without exception — is not soft. It is one of the Union's stiff-necked principles, ancient and deliberate. It binds the rich to the poor, the old settler to the newly arrived, and even former enemies to a single law. It is held even when it is costly, because the Union remembers what it cost when the threshold was closed to its founders.

## Civic order

The Union is governed by compact, witness, and public record. Town councils keep the ordinary burdens of road, levy, market, and relief; the higher courts of Sayyon hear disputes that cross town, corps, or charter. No office is held beyond judgment merely because it is old, wealthy, learned, or victorious.

To enter the compact is not to receive a favour. The newcomer is named before witnesses, receives the protection of the courts, and takes on the duties of any citizen: contribution according to means, service when called, and answerability for harm done. A former enemy who lays down arms may be received in this manner. The law does not require the people to forget what was done; it requires them to judge the person before them by oath and deed rather than by inherited stain.

These practices are tested most severely when grain is short, a gate is crowded, or an old grievance seeks a new victim. In such moments the Union's magistrates are expected to be sternest toward their own convenience. A threshold that opens only in easy years is no law at all.

## The Eighth-Day Severance

For the Halfelven kindreds of the Union, the rejection of inherited magical privilege is not only a legal doctrine; it is sealed in the flesh during the first days of life. When the Union's founders broke from the elder Elvish courts, they severed their ancestral bond to the Faerie plane — the source of Elvish longevity and innate sorcery. Left unsealed, a body of Elvish descent whose faerie tether is frayed begins to waste and consume itself.

To anchor each generation to mortal life and free judgment, the Union observes the **Eighth-Day Severance**:

- **The Seven Days of Quiet**: For the first seven days after birth, the newborn is kept with its family in a shaded, non-arcane chamber. Throughout these days, the mother drinks a measured draught of finely ground **Zoelite**, passing a gentle, dampening conductance to the infant through her milk (*smelting-milk*).
- **The Catharite Bath and the Crown-Seal**: Upon the eighth day, the child undergoes the Purification. The infant is bathed in a warm, dense solution of liquefied **Akosite** (*the Catharite bath*) to purge residual faerie energy clinging to the body. Because an infant's skull plates have not yet closed, physicians of the corps apply a conductive poultice of pulverized Akosite directly over the soft crown (the fontanelle), drawing the volatile remnant of the Faerie plane out of the developing mind.

The crossing is severe. For nearly all infants, the Akosite seals their frame and anchors them permanently to the mortal plane; yet for a rare few whose hearts are woven too deeply with residual faerie essence, the shock of the bath proves fatal. The Union does not regard this rite as a curse, but as the hard-won price of mortal independence — a shield against inherited dominion. In Sayyon's law, the eighth day is a sacred constitutional boundary: before the eighth day, the child belongs to nature; after the eighth day, untethered from the Faerie plane, the child is entered into the public rolls as a full, protected member of the Liminal Union.

## Civic learning

What the magi withheld behind initiation rites and sealed grimoires, the Union placed in schoolrooms open to every child — noble or lowborn, wealthy or destitute, long-settled or newly arrived across the threshold.

Reading and writing are not privileges in the Union's law. They are the minimum equipment of a free citizen, transmissible to all, withheld from none. The Union treats an illiterate citizen as a deprivation as serious as a broken wall: a gap in the commonwealth's fabric that the commonwealth is obligated to repair.

From the earliest settlements, the Union bound itself to the instruction of its people as firmly as it bound itself to the Ban on Living Spellcraft. The two are companion policies: where sorcery is forbidden, knowledge must be made universal, or the gap becomes a weakness. The **Halls of Inquiry** in Sayyon stand as the oldest institutions of higher learning on the Great Continent — open by merit and civic standing, not by bloodline or magical gift.

The people of the Union are not born more capable than their neighbours. They are, by long custom, better taught. Generals read law. Artificers study natural philosophy. Marines are drilled in navigation and civic obligation before they are drilled in arms. The scholar carries a defense obligation alongside the soldier; the soldier is expected to read.

This is not pride. It is policy, ancient and deliberate.

## Lawful artifice and the canonical alloys

The Union does not deny arcane force. It denies the right of privileged persons to wield it above the law.

For that reason it forbids living spellcraft, hereditary magical sovereignty, mage-schools, grimoires, and necromancy. Yet it permits arcane matter bound within lawful vessels: Arcanite, wargear, bound cores, and the old craft of warforms. What others entrust to magi, Sayyon entrusts to craft, inspection, and public law.

Across Irdya, high metallurgy and alchemy already stand beside spellcraft — as when Dwarvish runesmiths bind elemental force into physical weapons through hammer and structural mark, or when the alchemists of the southern sands compound naphtha and fire. The foundries of Sayyon carry this principle to its sternest conclusion, refining six canonical alloys and mineral compounds:

- **Pyrite (Fire)**: Mined from deep, volatile underground seams where ordinary fools' gold gives way to a fiercely reactive grade, Pyrite is forged into Maiden wargear and ground into sling-shot and lance-tips. Upon impact, it triggers a self-sustaining thermal reaction, dealing fire damage without a mage's incantation.
- **Kryonite (Cold)**: Drawn from deep-earth mineral beds, Kryonite is an ultra-dense, heat-drinking alloy that naturally absorbs and traps ambient warmth. Weapons and Marine shields wrought from Kryonite cast no frost-spell; rather, they drain thermal energy upon contact, chilling flesh and armour while bracing the shield-wall.
- **Arcanite (Arcane)**: Where Dwarvish smiths carve runes upon the surface of a blade, the Union's artificers melt and bind anti-sorcerous crystals directly into the metal during smelting. An Arcanite blade or warform plate acts as a natural harmonic disruptor to sorcery, sundering the dark magic that sustains an undead skeleton or the primal arcane vitality of a drake.
- **Uranite (Conductance)**: Borne in sealed officer rods and uniform vessels, Uranite does not blaze like a mage's beacon; instead, it radiates a steady conductance that tempers the harsh extremes of full Day and deep Night, anchoring liminal soldiers in twilight balance.
- **Zoelite and Akosite (Vitality and Remedy)**: In the deep iron deposits of ancient forest mountains, centuries of exposure to faerie-touched woodland waters infused the ore with the anti-venom and purifying properties of the surrounding flora. From this infused iron the Union refines **Zoelite** (a gentle, life-steadying mineral of dampening conductance) and **Akosite** (a sharp, purifying medicinal alloy). Beyond their use in the Eighth-Day Severance, field medics and keepers wear rings and conduits of Zoelite and Akosite that react to the vital body-heat of **Phoinix Messengers** — acting not as sources of magic, but as passive metallic vessels that amplify and project the bird's natural detoxifying presence.

## The Scarlet Faerie Stone

In Union memory, the Ruby of Fire is the **Scarlet Faerie Stone**. The name reflects both danger and distance: an object studied, feared, and never treated lightly. The annals of Sayyon remember the affair not as a tale of kingship, but as a necromantic crisis watched through merchants, couriers, scholars, and sealed intelligence.

## The public corps

The armies of the Union are public corps, not feudal retinues.

### Maiden Corps
The Maidens are the Union’s precision arm and anti-sorcerous edge: mobile, exact, and trusted with difficult work. Their officers hold rank by merit, oath, and law. In the annals they are also keepers of difficult passages: sent where a threatened settlement must be reached, a dangerous artifice secured, or an unlawful practitioner brought before the courts rather than left to private vengeance.

### Marine Corps
The Marines keep coasts, crossings, ports, and river-gates. If the Maidens are the blade, the Marines are the shield. Their duty begins before battle: convoying vulnerable ships, keeping the river records, preserving evacuation routes, and holding the walls long enough for civilians and archives to pass beyond danger.

### G9 Warforms
In lore-facing prose, the Union’s G9s should be treated as **warforms**, **Quickbound Golems**, or **Corebound Warforms**: ancient martial artifices of bound core and lawful craft, not science-fiction machines. They stand in the place the Union refuses to grant to a privileged battle-mage: force is made answerable to inspection, maintenance, record, and civic command. A warform may be formidable, but its core, repair, and deployment remain matters of public law.

### Phoinix Messengers
The Phoinix are living emblems of renewal, transition, and memory. They are not gods. They are also not pets or mascots. Their place in Union life is symbolic, civic, and military at once: they bear warning between dispersed forces, guide travellers through dangerous crossings, and preserve the chain of witness when ordinary messengers cannot pass.

## Foreign powers

The Union judges other powers chiefly by how power is ordered.

Among the northern peoples, the folk of the Knalgan holds and outlaw havens are most readily understood in Sayyon. Their mountain halls know the discipline of stone, furnace, ledger, and guarded road; their havens know the price paid by those whom greater realms leave outside their law. Neither people mistakes affinity for submission. Yet a charter kept, a caravan protected, and a warform honestly wrought are arguments they can weigh.

Thus the Union finds in the Knalgan lands not a brother-state, nor an untroubled ally, but a neighbour with whom compact and trade may endure: metals for sealed craft, timber and provisions for the passes, and guarded exchange across uncertain roads.

It sees the **High Courts of Evercrown** as ancient and subtle, yet bound to blood and inherited gift.

Its dealings with those courts are most difficult at the forest margins, where passage, timber, and the standing of mixed kindreds may become questions of inherited right. Sayyoni envoys keep the forms of courtesy, but resist any settlement that turns descent into a title above the compact.

It sees the **Crown-Realms of the Burning Standard** as lawful near-kindred, yet compromised by crown, sanctified power, and tolerated magi.

At river ports and against common raiders, officers of the Crown-Realms and Union captains have sometimes found reason to cooperate. Such agreements endure only while the Crown's officers accept that Sayyon's citizens answer to charter and court, not to a distant throne or consecrated hierarchy.

It sees the **Emirates of the Veiled Moon** as disciplined and capable, yet constrained by sacred hierarchy and inherited standing.

Their merchants and the Union's have met most profitably over well-charters, caravan passage, and the repair of roads through hard country. These bargains are exacting, for each side brings an old law to the table; neither readily yields the right to name whose law governs a stranger in need.

It sees the **Horde-Realms of the Iron Banner** as strength without law.

It sees **the Dominion** as caste and blood hardened into public order.

The Union knows the Dominion chiefly through those who escape it. Their arrival at the southern gates is treated as evidence to be heard by the courts, not as a license for indiscriminate revenge; yet the testimony of the displaced has made Sayyon wary of every order that calls a person's birth a permanent sentence.

It sees the **Sun-Scar Necrodominion** as a metaphysical affront to renewal and civic life: not merely a rival, but an enemy beyond concord.

No compact is sought with the Sun-Scar. Its approaches are recorded in sealed annals, its agents watched, and its relics contained or destroyed under public authority. Where other rivals may be met by envoy or caravan, necromantic doctrine is met by vigilance and refusal.

It sees the **Red Dawn Commonwealth** as equality hardened into doctrine.

If the vampire covenant remains in use, the Union treats the vampiric houses as corrupted and dangerous, yet distinct from necromantic doctrine and tolerable only under strict law.

### The Treaty of the Living and the Bound

The Union's strangest and most severely regulated compact is that held with the vampiric houses of the Dark Forest. In the ancient Sundering from the Elvish courts, the democratic Halfelves and a faction of occultist Elvish exiles fled together as allies of convenience before their paths diverged. Where the Halfelves chose mortal finitude and the Eighth-Day Severance, the occultists refused to surrender their immortality; when their tie to the Faerie plane broke and curdled, their bodies began to consume themselves, forcing them to sustain their flesh through forbidden blood-ritual.

Three stern realities bind Sayyon and the Dark Forest in uneasy truce:

- **The Dark Forest Charter**: A controversial foundational provision — the **Treaty of the Living and the Bound** — recognizes the vampiric houses as a separate, sovereign border enclave within the Dark Forest. There they stand as a grim buffer zone, slaughtering monarchist task-forces sent from the High Courts of Evercrown to reclaim the southern marches. In return, the Union grants them strictly policed scientific asylum, medical counsel from corps physicians, and the chartered right to hunt the wild megafauna of the borderlands, provided no citizen or traveller under Union law is ever touched.
- **The Galvanic Spark and the Siphoned Pulse**: The blood-rituals by which the vampires anchor their unstable bodies generate a potent, steady bio-resonance. Under compact, Union artificers fashion sealed steel vessels and filtration conduits that allow the vampiric houses to store harvested animal life-force cleanly without decay. In return, that bound bio-resonance is tapped to charge the dense conductance cells housed within the **Uranite** rods and uniforms of Union officers — binding the sunlit commonwealth and the shadowed enclave in a strict dependency of lawful infrastructure.
- **The Blood-Iron Exchange**: Because the Union's own smiths will not practice living sorcery to harvest raw arcane matter, and because the vampires' forest-game reservoirs precipitate condensed, arcane-charged mineral byproducts, a heavily inspected trade endures at the border gates. Sayyon's engineers and medics deliver high-grade containment cages, medical filtration tubes, and hunting gear; the vampiric houses deliver the raw, charged mineral residues required to smelt volatile grades of **Pyrite** and **Arcanite**. It is a cold compact of mutual survival, kept without affection and watched by law at every step.
